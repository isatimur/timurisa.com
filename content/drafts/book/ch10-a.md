Omar Khattab built systems, DSPy among them, across years of model turnover. Chapter 10 reports his verdict: they "fundamentally stayed the same over the years," from text-davinci-002 up to o4-mini. The models underneath were swapped out entirely. The structure stayed.

That fact frames the chapter's question. Agent frameworks multiply. Tool protocols proliferate. New releases reorder yesterday's leaderboard. Interfaces move from chat windows to canvases to copilots to swarms. Some of that is real progress. The chapter calls some of it marketing theater with a GPU budget. So what endures?

The chapter's answer is not a framework, a model family or an orchestration fashion. It is a pattern for turning machine capability into dependable work.

## The pattern has a name

The chapter calls it constrained delegation. The machine gets a prepared environment, a clearer representation of intent and a workable slice of context. It gets a runtime that can preserve and expose state. Its authority matches the risk. Human review sits where it matters most.

The chapter also defines the pattern by what it excludes: raw generation, autonomous theater, tool sprawl disguised as capability, infinite context pretending to be memory, and a leaderboard screenshot standing in for product trust.

The chapter says the pattern will outlast today's frameworks and likely today's interface assumptions. The reason is simple. The pattern does not work around weak models. It answers the nature of delegated work itself.

## Look past the interface

The visible part of a system is not the stable part. A coding agent does not earn trust with a polished diff view. A realtime voice system does not earn trust with natural speech. The question underneath is the same each time: what must be true around the model for delegated work to deserve trust?

The chapter's answer is the environment. Prepared repositories, specs, validations, retrieval systems, memory layers, runtimes, permissions and review surfaces are not accessories. They are how intelligence becomes situated. What endures, the chapter says, is not prompt cleverness but environment design.

Stronger models did not remove this work. They moved it. The chapter says teams that expected better models to dissolve complexity found it migrating into context assembly, eval design, runtime semantics, authority boundaries and organizational throughput. The model grew more capable. The loop around it grew more consequential.

The chapter also rejects the maximalist story of full autonomy. The most useful systems in the book were rarely the ones with the least human involvement. They were the ones with the clearest handoffs. Autonomy is a variable to tune, not a trophy.

## Run the strike-out test

Take the plan for your next AI project. Strike out every model name and product name. Read what remains.

Does the plan still name a prepared environment, explicit intent, bounded authority and focused review? Then it rests on the parts the chapter calls durable. Does nothing remain? Then the plan depends on a moment, and the next release may end it.

Do this on the plan that sits open on your desk today. Rewrite one line that names a product so it names the constraint instead.
